The data is published, and the map is no longer French.

• 306 networks in 35 countries, every one that publishes its stations as open data: Prague, Barcelona, Riga, Pristina, Buenos Aires. You choose your city on first launch, and none is served by default.
• The datasets are downloadable at last. 101 cities are ready — the seventy French ones and thirty-one others — and no longer have to be produced from the repository's scripts.
• Address search works outside France: where there is no national address base, the streets and house numbers are read from OpenStreetMap itself, in the language of the country.
• The map stops at the edge of what was downloaded rather than showing an empty void beyond it.
• A journey's two ends stay on the result screen and can be corrected there.
• Distances are shown in the units your region uses: miles and feet in the United States, miles and yards in the United Kingdom, kilometres everywhere else. The setting is in Settings if you want otherwise.
• You say how fast you walk — slow, normal or brisk — and the journey is worked out for it. Walking slowly can change the departure station you are offered: a nearer one, at the price of pedalling a little further. "Normal" is what the application did until now, and it is the default.
• You choose the screen the application opens on: the map, as before, or the station list. Opening on the list asks for no location permission at all.
• The bike and dock counts can be shown larger, from Settings. The application already follows the text size set on your phone; this enlarges that one figure without enlarging everything else. The map markers keep their size, so stations stay legible side by side.
• You can say whether your own bike is mechanical or electric. It changes how a journey on your own bike is drawn and described, and nothing else: no route and no time depends on it.

Every city's weight is announced before downloading: 3 MB for Auray, 143 MB for Paris.
