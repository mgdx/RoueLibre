The maps answer the hand, and you can name your home and your work.

• Both maps turn under two fingers and tilt, up to forty-five degrees. A compass shows up when the map is off north or off flat, and one press gives back both.
• A home and a work, named once in the settings, head the shortcuts when you say where a journey starts or ends. They are yours to erase in one press, and nothing you go to is ever kept.
• A place found on the map opens a sheet of what to do with it: leave from here, go there, hand it to a navigation application, clear it.
• Five languages more — Basque, Catalan, Chinese, Galician and Serbian — thirty in all.
• "Download only on Wi-Fi" replaces "on an unmetered connection", and the warnings no longer claim a bill your plan may not charge.
• A point outside the covered area is now told from the other end of the journey, the handover button is no longer cut by the edge of the screen, addresses read out of a shared text are offered as a guess rather than announced as a finding, and the storage screen says a download has begun and remembers one that failed.
