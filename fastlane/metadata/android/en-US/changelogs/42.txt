The first version published, and every city's data is ready.

• All 331 networks in 38 countries now have their data produced and published — no longer a hundred of them. Whichever city you choose, its map, its streets and its addresses are there to download.
• The application speaks 26 languages, chosen in the settings whatever language your phone is in.
• You can ask for a mechanical bike or an electric one, and the journey is worked out for that kind. It is a filter and not a preference: a station holding none of that kind is not offered at all, and when none is left nearby the answer says so instead of walking you towards a bike that is not there.
• A station says how many of its bikes are mechanical and how many are electric — a cog for the first, a bolt for the second. Where the figures do not add up, the total stands alone rather than a split nobody could verify.
• A journey says what it climbs, in metres, over the whole trip and step by step. A share bike is heavy, and a hill is worth knowing about before setting off.
• The summary ends on the bikes waiting at the departure station, the very counts the journey was worked out on.
• The ride draws the ground it runs over, the journey opens full screen, and a button brings the map back onto the whole of it.
• Any leg can be handed to a navigation application, for the turn-by-turn guidance this one deliberately does not give.
• The application has an icon of its own — a bicycle whose front wheel is a map — and the interface takes its colour.

9.8 MB of APK, and every city's weight still announced before the download.
